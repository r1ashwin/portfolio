Ashwin Deshpande · Qekto

Hey, I’m Ashwin. I’m 23 and I’m building Qekto.

I’m a software engineer at Amazon, where right after my internship they picked me as one of two founding engineers for a new Prime Video Ads team, which handles ads data worldwide. Back in college, I published an ML research paper, I ran a small hardware startup where I sold electromechanical clocks, drones, custom PCBs and small electronics people asked me to build, and I was the lead of my university’s robotics hub for two years, where I raised over three lakh rupees by running workshops to keep the hub going.

Coming back to the present, my office workload has been really crazy, so I ended up building a few AI agents to help myself and take some load off. Out of curiosity, one day I thought, why not switch them to local models just to test them? It turned out they were really, really good, even on big multimodal workflows. That’s when I realized that unless the work is truly creative, maybe most companies don’t need frontier models anymore. They can use open models, stop depending on frontier labs, and own their models and tools entirely.

Today, most companies still build everything on frontier models, because building on their own needs a proper ML team. What’s hard is everything around the model: understanding the workflow, collecting the right data, the training part of it, trusting the model enough to ship it, and actually keeping it running as models, products and situations change. That takes a whole ML team.

So I built Qekto to be that ML team. It runs on a company’s own servers and learns their tasks from their AI traffic. It builds tests from real usage, trains a model for each task, and switches over only when that model beats what they use today. After that, it keeps watching every model in production, and retrains or upgrades it whenever results start getting worse or a better open model comes out.

I built a working version in two days and shared it with developer friends at startups and big tech companies to get early feedback. I’ve also started reaching out to startup founders and CTOs to find the first teams to use Qekto. This is Qekto v1. Let me show you.
